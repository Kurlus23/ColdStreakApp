Ok we need to flesh out the trivia system.  It's a cold plunge app so I'd like it to focus around teaching people about plunging, but not so strict that everything has to be cold plunging

Absolutely. I think the trivia should have a ColdStreak personality, not become a “cold plunge certification exam.” The sweet spot is probably about 25–35% cold/wellness related and 65–75% general trivia.

That gives people a reason to learn something about what they're doing while still making Brain Freeze feel like an actual trivia game.

🧠 Brain Freeze — Trivia Categories

I'd structure the question pool into tiers.

🧊 1. Cold & Plunge — ~25%

This is the category that makes Brain Freeze belong inside ColdStreak.

Topics could include:

Cold-water immersion
Thermoregulation
Vasoconstriction/vasodilation
Shivering
Brown fat
Heart rate
Recovery
Exercise physiology
Historical cold exposure
Ice swimming
Polar exploration
Cold-weather survival
Famous cold locations
Interesting cold facts

But importantly, not every question needs to be scientifically serious.

For example:

What causes the familiar shivering response when you're cold?

A. Your muscles rapidly contract and relax
B. Your blood becomes thicker
C. Your heart beats slower
D. Your lungs produce heat

That's educational.

But then:

Which U.S. state has the coldest recorded temperature?

A. Alaska
B. Minnesota
C. Montana
D. North Dakota

That's still "cold" without being about plunging.

🌡️ 2. Human Body — ~15%

This fits the app extremely well without constantly talking about plunges.

Brain

Human performance

This could be your higher-value question category.

❄️ And then I'd introduce "Cold Facts"

This is a separate mechanic rather than a category.

Every so often, after answering a question, you get a 10-second fact card.

For example:

🧊 COLD FACT

Your body doesn't actually "warm the water."

Water conducts heat away from your body much faster than air at the same temperature.

+50 XP

Then:

NEXT QUESTION →

This is where you can teach people about cold plunging without turning every question into cold-plunge trivia.

The really important part: question frequency

With a question every 30 seconds, I wouldn't make the categories completely random.

I'd create a Brain Freeze recipe.

For a 5-minute game = 10 questions:

Q1: General
Q2: Cold
Q3: Science/body
Q4: Pop culture
Q5: General
Q6: Cold
Q7: History/geography
Q8: Body/fitness
Q9: General
Q10: Cold

So roughly 3 of 10 questions are directly related to the ColdStreak world.

That feels intentional without becoming repetitive.

But there's another opportunity: difficulty should be personalized

I'd track how people perform.

If someone gets:

Easy → correct
Easy → correct
Medium → correct

Brain Freeze can gradually push them toward harder questions.

But if they're getting destroyed:

Easy → wrong
Medium → wrong

Don't punish them.

Give them another accessible question.

So after a few plunges, Brain Freeze learns:

Kevin is really good at history but terrible at geography.

Then it can subtly adjust the mix.

That creates another reason to come back.

And this ties directly into the plunge benefits

This is where I think Brain Freeze could become really distinctive.

When someone chooses:

🎯 Mood

they might get a few questions related to the brain/nervous system.

Choosing:

🔥 Metabolism

could increase the probability of metabolism/body questions.

Choosing:

💪 Recovery

could bring in muscle/exercise questions.

Not only those questions.

Just a slight thematic bias.

So the benefit goal isn't just changing the timer.

It changes the flavor of the game.

One thing I'd avoid

I would not have Brain Freeze constantly tell people that cold plunging is amazing.

That will get old fast.

Instead, make the questions genuinely interesting and scientifically responsible.

For example, instead of:

"How much does cold plunging increase your metabolism?"

(which gets into questionable claims)

Ask:

What is the body's first-line response to sudden cold exposure?

or:

Why does cold water feel much colder than air at the same temperature?

or:

Which tissue is particularly important for generating heat without shivering?

You're teaching people how cold exposure works, rather than selling them on it.

That gives ColdStreak credibility.

I'd ultimately shoot for something like this:

Brain Freeze's 1,000-question pool

🧊 250 Cold / Cold Exposure
🧠 150 Human Body / Physiology
🔬 150 Science
🌎 150 General Knowledge
🎬 125 Pop Culture
🏈 75 Sports
🏛️ 50 History
🧩 50 Brain Busters

Then the algorithm builds each person's run from that pool.

And importantly, the 250 cold questions become ColdStreak's moat.

A generic trivia app can give you:

"Who painted the Mona Lisa?"

But Brain Freeze can give you:

You're sitting in 39°F water right now. Why is your body constricting blood vessels near your skin?

That's the moment where the game and the app actually become one product, rather than ColdStreak just having a random trivia game bolted onto it.

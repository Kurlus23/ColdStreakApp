Honestly, this is pretty good. I’d give it about an 8/10 for a first onboarding email.

It does a good job explaining what ColdStreak actually does without being overly long. But I’d make a few changes because the email should do more than explain features—it should get the new user to open the app and do their first plunge.

What I like
“Welcome to your coldest habit.” — good headline. It feels like a brand rather than generic app copy.
The numbered structure makes it very easy to scan.
It hits almost all of ColdStreak's major features.
Brain Freeze gets prominent placement, which I like because that's one of your differentiators.
The Bluetooth thermometer and music integrations are good things to mention.
The medical disclaimer is appropriately restrained rather than dominating the email.
“Start small…” gives the user an immediate action.
What I'd change

1. Don't lead with five features. Lead with the first action.

A brand-new user doesn't need to understand ColdStreak yet. You want:

Open the app → start a plunge → see your Cold Score → come back tomorrow.

The current email feels slightly like a product manual.

I'd make the first section something like:

Your first plunge takes less than a minute to set up.
Open ColdStreak, set your water temperature, start the timer, and jump in. We'll take care of the tracking.

Then introduce the other features underneath.

2. “Body metrics” may be confusing.

“Add your water temperature and optional body metrics to calculate your Cold Score.”

If users don't immediately know what “body metrics” means, that's friction. I'd either say exactly what they are or leave that out.

3. Brain Freeze should probably be more exciting.

This is one of the things I'd lean into harder.

Instead of:

“Answer quick trivia questions during a plunge to earn points and cold-water bonuses.”

Something more like:

Turn your plunge into a game.
Brain Freeze throws quick trivia questions at you while you're in the water. Answer correctly, earn points, and challenge your friends.

That makes it sound fun rather than another feature to learn.

4. The Explore section is doing too much.

“Submit a spot, browse public profiles, join events, and climb local leaderboards” is a lot for someone who just created an account.

I'd keep the community angle but simplify it:

Find your people.
Discover plunge spots, events, and other cold-plunge enthusiasts near you. Compete on leaderboards or challenge friends.

5. The CTA could be much stronger.

“Open ColdStreak” is fine, but I'd make the CTA reinforce the first plunge:

Start Your First Plunge

That's the behavior you actually want.

One other thing I'd seriously consider

The username “Kurlus23” jumps out at me.

If that's the user's ColdStreak username, that's fine. But I'd make sure Replit is pulling the display name the user actually chose, rather than an automatically generated username. A welcome email saying:

Hey Kevin,

feels much more personal than:

Hey Kurlus23,

if that's not the name they identify with.

Overall

The email is good enough to ship, but I wouldn't consider it the final version.

I'd shift it from:

“Here are all the things ColdStreak can do.”

to:

“Welcome → here's why you're going to like this → do your first plunge → here's what you'll discover as you keep using it.”

That would make it feel considerably more like a consumer fitness app onboarding email and less like a feature overview.

And importantly, I would not make it much longer. The current length is about right; I'd just redistribute the emphasis.
